# Measurements: why SAW costs so much more on the spec layer (#69)

Measured 2026-10-21/22 on the x86_64 IOD (`ip-10-10-103-187`: 8 CPUs,
15.7 GB, no swap), on `main` = `09fa337` (PR #93's merge). The SAW record
`spark/saw/results.txt` is of `937e485`; nothing SAW reads changed since.
Tools as in `results.txt`: llvm-gcc 27.0 (LLVM 21.1.8), SAW 1.6, Cryptol
3.6.0, z3 4.8.14, SAW's yices.

**How.** Each experiment is a SAW script, `e_<name>.saw`, and, for the
experiments that compare two ways of writing one function, the probe
units `probes/*.ads` (not part of the crate, outside `spark/src`, never
proved by gnatprove), all under `spark/saw/experiments/issue-69/`.
`run.sh` runs one against the bitcode `check-saw.sh` builds, in a scratch
copy of `spark/saw/`'s scripts, optionally with one `sed` variant applied
(`variants/`), in a capped systemd scope, timed with `/usr/bin/time -v`
(wall-clock, peak RSS). `run-all.sh` runs them all, one saw at a time;
the numbers below are from one such run on an otherwise idle machine
(`final/`), unless marked "dev" (a first run, alongside another saw).
`causes.py` and `pairs.py` read a `check-saw.sh` record;
`profile_summary.py` reads crucible's profile. To reproduce:

```console
$ . /it/foundation-tools/env.sh            # GNAT_LLVM_DIR, LLVM_LINK_DIR, saw
$ SAW_LAYERS=spec spark/scripts/check-saw.sh --print   # builds spark/saw/obj
$ spark/saw/experiments/issue-69/run-all.sh /tmp/issue-69
$ python3 -I spark/saw/experiments/issue-69/causes.py spark/saw/results.txt
```

Two things appear only in the experiments, never in a recorded script:
the tactic `admit`, which accepts a goal without a solver (a proof timed
with it is symbolic execution, override matching and goal construction;
the solver's share is the difference), and crucible's profiler
(`enable_crucible_profiling`).

**Skipped.** No new SAW record (`results.txt` is current: nothing SAW
reads changed), and the long record (`results-long.txt`, 7708 s) was not
re-run. No file under `spark/src` changed.

## The contrast, now

The issue's table was of 2026-10-19. Today's, from `results.txt`
(`937e485`) and a full `check-saw.sh --print` on `09fa337` with each saw
run's peak RSS recorded (`full-base/`, 2026-10-21; the sha256_spec run
shared the machine with dev experiments for part of its 39 min):

| | implementation | spec layer | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| results | 90 (24 lax) | 2800 (1396 lax) | 31 |
| SAW's summed proof times (`results.txt`) | 32.6 s | 2648 s | 81 |
| scripts' wall-clock (`results.txt`) | 36 s (aes 5, sha256 31) | 2730 s (aes 393, sha256 2337) | 76 |
| scripts' wall-clock (`full-base`) | 36 s (4.4 + 31.2) | 2750 s (398 + 2352) | 76 |
| peak RSS (`full-base`) | 0.21 GB (aes), 0.30 GB (sha256) | 1.36 GB (aes), 3.89 GB (sha256) | 13 |
| mean time per result | 0.36 s | 0.95 s | 2.6 |

The "100×" is now 76× in time. **It is mostly a count, 31× more results,
and then 2.6× more per result.** The rest of this file is why.

### The same Cryptol function, proved both ways

`pairs.py` sets each Cryptol function's implementation results (with the
leaves they rest on) against the spec layer's, from `results.txt`. HMAC's
row takes the hashes' lemmas proved at HMAC's secondary-stack bases.

| Cryptol | impl results | impl s | spec results | spec s | time ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SHA-256 `step` (compression) | 40 | 6.41 | 522 | 95.44 | 15 |
| SHA-256 `hash` (`pad`, `parse`, `foldl step`) | 12 | 7.52 | 576 | 1245.62 | 166 |
| HMAC-SHA-256 | 7 | 15.55 | 553 | 929.86 | 60 |
| AES `KeyExpansion` | 4 | 1.06 | 159 | 75.94 | 72 |
| AES `Cipher`, `InvCipher` (and `InvCipher'` on the spec side) | 18 | 1.80 | 132 | 93.17 | 52 |
| AES S-box, GF(2^8) | 5 | 0.18 | 824 | 189.92 | 1055 |

The implementation's `Compress` is one proof of 64 rounds (2.8 s); the
spec's `step` is 64 `W` lemmas, 64 `T1`, 64 `Step3`, 65 fold lemmas and
`Step`, in each of two loads. The implementation's `Expand_Key` is one
proof (0.3 s per key size); the spec's is 44, 52 or 60 `W` lemmas and
`KeyExpansion`. Where the two are written alike (the leaves: `SubBytes`,
`ShiftRows`, `MixColumns`, `Round`, the sigmas) they cost the same,
0.02 to 0.09 s each.

## Where the spec layer's time goes

`causes.py` puts each spec-layer result of `results.txt` in one group,
the first that matches (2800 results, 2648 s summed):

| group | what | results | time | share of time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stack-base | a lemma proved again only because the secondary stack's base, or the bytes kept below it, differ: `To_Bits`' chain (65 lemmas) after the first, 14 times; `Hash`, `Pad`, `Zero` at HMAC's bases; `HMAC_init` at its second | 937 | 1175 s | **44.4%** |
| per-index | a lemma at one index or count of a recursion: `Word_Of` (66, 599 s), the first `To_Bits` chain (65, 57 s), AES `W` (156, 64 s), the cipher folds (108, 87 s), SHA-2's `W`, `T1`, `Step3`, `Rounds.Foldl` (257, 46 s), GF folds (45, 14 s), `Foldl_Take` (5) | 702 | 868 s | **32.8%** |
| top | one result per function and size (`Hash` 125 s, `Pad` 88 s, `HMAC` 60 s, `HMAC_init` 46 s, `Parse` 19 s, ...) | 126 | 382 s | 14.4% |
| per-value | the exhaustive `Pgcd`, `Inverse`, `Affine`, one proof per byte | 768 | 175 s | 6.6% |
| lax-copy | the compression function's lemmas proved again in the load with lax arithmetic | 267 | 48 s | 1.8% |

The groups are disjoint, but the causes are not: a `To_Bits` lemma at
HMAC's 9968-byte base exists because `To_Bits` recurses (65 lemmas, not
one), because its result is on the secondary stack (a chain per base),
and because a null range forces the lax load. The hypotheses below each
measure one cause with the others held fixed, and give its share as what
removing it would remove.

## The hypotheses

### 1. Recursion, a lemma per index — confirmed, with a correction

**Confirmed:** the per-index lemmas are 702 results and 32.8% of the time
(table above), and the recursions multiply the stack-base group too (each
re-proof is a chain of 65).

**The correction: for a recursion with one recursive call, the lemmas
cost more than letting SAW unfold it.** The spec layer's rule ("recursions
are never unfolded", README "SAW") was set for `KeyExpansion.W`; measured
on each recursion (`e_recursion_*.saw`, same contracts, overrides and
tactics as the recorded lemmas; dev numbers, the final run's below them):

| recursion | calls | per-index lemmas (all) | unfolded, one proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Rounds.Foldl` (SHA-2's 64 rounds, Step3's lemmas as overrides) | one | 65 lemmas, 23.4 s | N = 64: **2.4 s** (8: 0.29, 16: 0.47, 32: 0.98) |
| `To_Bits` (`Literal`, stack at 48) | one | 65 lemmas, 43.2 s (record: 53.8 s) | W = 64: **1.4 s** (8: 0.66, 16: 0.83, 32: 0.94) |
| `To_Bits`, probe `Literal_Agg` (no recursion: an iterated aggregate) | none | | W = 64: **0.78 s** |
| `Word_Of` (`Value`, P of 512 bits, First symbolic) | one | 33 lemmas, 177 s (record) | Count = 8: **238 s**; Count = 16: stopped after 22 min |
| AES `W` at k = 128 | two (I - 1, I - 4) | 44 lemmas, 16.7 s | I = 16: 0.34 s, 18: 0.47, 20: 0.59; calls grow 1.38× per index: 1,142,775 at I = 43, 4,147,840 for `KeyExpansion` |
| SHA-2 `W` | four | 64 lemmas, 7.7 s | T = 16..22: 0.10 s; 64,977 calls at T = 63 |

So the lemmas are **necessary** where the recursion has more than one
call (the spec's `W`s: Cryptol's `w` and `W` are streams, evaluated once
each; a SPARK recursive function has no sharing, so executing it is
exponential), and where unfolding makes the solver's goal blow up
(`Word_Of`, below: a symbolic index per level). They are **a choice that
costs 10 to 40×** for the linear folds and `To_Bits`: unfolding
`Rounds.Foldl` and `To_Bits` would turn 130 + 975 lemmas into a few
proofs. That choice, not recursion itself, is most of the per-index
group outside `Word_Of`.

### 2. The secondary stack — confirmed: the largest share

**Confirmed, 44.4% of the time (937 results), and a per-proof overhead
on top.** A spec function returning an unconstrained array (`Bits`,
`Round_Keys` slices) allocates its result on GNAT's secondary stack. The
contract must say where: at a concrete offset of the stack's chunk, with
the bytes the caller keeps below it stated unchanged (SAW drops the bytes
of a writable allocation that an override's postcondition does not
state). So an override applies only at the stack state its contract
names, and a lemma is proved again for each caller's base. That is the
stack-base group: `To_Bits`' chain proved 15 times (7 for `Pad`'s length
groups, whose `Zero` results differ in size, two of them at the same
480-byte base with different bytes kept; 8 for HMAC's calls of `Hash`),
plus `Hash`, `Pad`, `Zero` and `HMAC_init` at HMAC's bases. **SAW 1.6
cannot state a lemma at a symbolic base**: its setup language places a
value only at a constant offset of an allocation (`llvm_elem`,
`llvm_field` take constants; the full list of `llvm_*` commands has no
pointer arithmetic on terms), so the re-proofs are forced.

Measured, the per-proof overhead (`e_tobits_w0b.saw`, `admit`, dev): the
`To_Bits` lemma at W = 0 (an empty result) takes 0.36 s with its stack
contract, 0.20 s without a postcondition, 0.24 s with only the result's
bounds stated, 0.32 to 0.37 s with only the stack header restated;
`Small_K` (no stack) 0.03 s. Crucible's profile of that lemma: **2.3 ms
of symbolic execution**, `SS_Allocate` included (`e_tobits_w0.saw`). So
the runtime's code costs nothing; the stack *contract* (setting up the
chunk, the soft link and `NT_TSD`, and matching the restated header and
kept bytes afterwards) costs about 0.3 s a proof. The chunk's size does
not matter: the 65-lemma chain takes 43.2 s at 64 KiB, 43.3 s at 8 KiB
(`variants/chunk8k.sed`) and (final run) at 1 MiB. In `Hash`, the
runtime's three allocations take 6 ms of the 1.48 s simulated.

The same function with and without the stack (probes, 64-bit index): the
iterated aggregate `Literal_Agg` at W = 64 on the secondary stack 0.59 s,
`Literal_64`, the same aggregate returning the constrained `Bits (0 ..
63)` through a hidden pointer, **0.18 s**, and a lemma about it serves
every caller.

### 3. Bit sequences as Boolean arrays; `Word_Of` growing with length — confirmed

**Confirmed: `Word_Of` is 599 s (22.6%) for 66 lemmas, and the Boolean
array, one byte per bit, is about 94% of it.** Each `Word_Of` lemma reads
one bit at a symbolic position of P, and its contract states P's bytes
(each 0 or 1) and the Cryptol `zext (take (p << first))`. Probe `Value`
(word for word the prelude's `Join_Split.Value`) against `Value_Packed`
(the same recursion over the same bits packed eight to a byte):

| P | `Value`, Boolean bytes, 33 lemmas | `Value_Packed`, 33 lemmas |
|---|---|---|
| 512 bits | 185 s (5.5 s a lemma) | **12.0 s** |
| 1024 bits | 405 s (12.3 s a lemma) | **17.1 s** |
| 2048 bits | (record: 27 s a lemma) | final run |

Where the time goes in one lemma (`e_hash.saw`, `Word_Of` at Count = 32,
512 bits): yices 5.4 s, z3 7.8 s, `admit` **0.24 s**: 95% is the solver,
relating a symbolic index into 512 separately constrained bytes to a
symbolic shift of a 512-bit vector. The packed P has 64 bytes and no
validity precondition. With First concrete (as `Parse` calls it, at 32 j)
the lemma takes (final run). The 128-bit index is not the cost: the
`To_Bits` chain takes 40.7 s at a 128-bit index and 38.5 s at a 64-bit
one (probes, dev), and `Value` (final run).

The Boolean array also costs where a lemma is *used*: in `Hash` at 55
bytes, applying `Word_Of`'s lemma 16 times takes 1.22 s of the 1.48 s of
symbolic execution (crucible's profile, `overrideBranches`): each
application checks the 512 bytes against the lemma's precondition.

### 4. Null ranges force the lax module — confirmed as the cause; a small share

**Confirmed:** probe `Literal` (the prelude's `To_Bits`, word for word),
loaded without lax arithmetic, fails at W = 1: "Poison value created:
Unsigned addition caused wrapping even though the `nuw` flag was set",
counterexample `v = 0`. `Literal_No_Null`, the same function with W = 1 a
case of its own (no concatenation with a null left operand), proves its
whole chain in the strict load (42.9 s for 65 lemmas, against `Literal`'s
40.7 s in the lax load): the null range alone forces the lax load.

**Its share is small: 1.8% (48 s).** The second load itself costs 0.02 s
(`e_hash.saw`: 0.06 s strict, 0.02 s lax; SAW translates lazily); the
cost is the compression function's 267 lemmas proved again in it, because
an override must come from the module being verified. Lax arithmetic does
not slow a proof: the same 267 lemmas take 47.9 s strict and 47.8 s lax
(`results.txt`). What
it does cost is assurance, not time: in the 1396 lax results absence of
overflow rests on gnatprove.

### 5. What else the experiments turned up

* **Recursion on a value: 768 results, 175 s (6.6%).** GF(2)'s `pgcd`
  recurses on a degree, not a size, and SAW's evaluation of the NSA
  Cryptol's `inner` on a symbolic byte does not terminate (README "SAW"):
  so `Pgcd`, `Inverse` and `Affine` are proved byte by byte. This is the
  Cryptol's recursion, not SPARK's (not re-measured here).
* **Most of a spec proof is the solver, not symbolic execution**
  (`e_hash.saw`, `admit`): `Word_Of` 95% solver, `Parse` 96% (3.72 s, of
  which 0.14 s without the solver), `Pad` 59% (1.76 s, 0.72 s), `Hash`
  52% (4.88 s, 2.34 s). The exception is the stack lemmas (`To_Bits`:
  the solver is 7% of the chain, 43.2 s against 40.3 to 41.1 s with
  `admit`), whose cost is the contract, above.
* **A per-proof floor.** A proof's fixed cost is about 0.03 s for a
  scalar contract (`Small_K`), 0.1 s for SHA-2's `W` (a block by
  reference), 0.24 s for AES `W` (key words by reference), 0.36 to 0.6 s
  for a stack contract. At 2800 results this floor alone is several
  hundred seconds; the implementation has 90 results.
* **The proof context grows.** The recorded `To_Bits` chain at 48 bytes
  took 53.8 s inside `sha256_spec.saw`; the same chain alone took 43.2 s.
* **Not the cause:** the 128-bit index (above), the chunk size (above),
  the second module load (above).

## What this means for proving C-to-SPARK translation equivalence

The spec layer against the implementation is the same contrast as a
SPARK translation against the C it translates: code written to be read,
checked against code written to run, by symbolic execution of both
bitcodes. Which costs here would recur, and whose they are:

**SPARK's spec style** (choices in how the spec layer is written):
* **Unconstrained results.** Mirroring a size-polymorphic Cryptol
  function as an Ada function over unconstrained arrays puts every result
  on the secondary stack. With SAW's fixed-offset contracts this is the
  largest cost (44%): a lemma per caller's stack state.
* **Recursion without sharing.** Cryptol's `w` and `W` are streams; a
  recursive SPARK function recomputes them, exponentially if executed. A
  lemma per index is then unavoidable (cheap here: AES's 156 and SHA-2's
  128 `W` lemmas, both loads, 79 s).
* **Recursion where the Cryptol has none.** `To_Bits` (Cryptol's literal
  `` (`l : [64]) ``) and `Word_Of` (a word of `split`) recurse in the
  SPARK prelude, not in the Cryptol: their lemmas (1041, about 1580 s
  with their stack re-proofs) are SPARK's.
* **One byte per bit**, symbolically indexed: `Word_Of`, 14 to 24× the
  packed form.

**GNAT-LLVM's code generation:**
* the secondary stack, through the runtime's `SS_Allocate` and a soft
  link SAW cannot call as GNAT calls it (an adapter is needed);
* the null range's length computed with an `add nuw` that wraps in the
  discarded `select` arm (legal LLVM; crucible rejects it): the lax load;
* every expression function `alwaysinline` (`-fno-inline` needed to keep
  calls that lemmas can override), `-O0` only (SROA's `poison` at
  `-O1`), and an `i128` GEP index for a constrained array of a 128-bit
  index type (the probe's `Literal_64` at the 128-bit index has one).

None of these is a cost in time by itself: the runtime's code simulates in
milliseconds, and the lax load is free. They cost through SAW.

**SAW:**
* overrides match only a concrete memory layout, and its setup language
  has no symbolic offsets: every stack-allocated result needs a lemma per
  stack state;
* an applied override invalidates the writable memory its postcondition
  does not state, so a lemma must restate what each caller keeps;
* overrides come only from the module verified: lemmas are re-proved per
  load;
* it does not unfold a recursion with sharing, and its evaluation of a
  value-recursive Cryptol function on a symbolic argument does not
  terminate;
* a per-proof floor that a contract over large memory raises.

**What would make a spec layer cheaper for SAW without moving it from the
Cryptol** (each measured above on a probe or by unfolding; none applied
to `spark/src`, which this issue does not change):
1. **Constrained results at the instance's sizes.** Cryptol's types are
   fixed at each instance (`pad`'s `[64]`, `hash`'s `[l]`); a SPARK
   generic over those sizes, as `AES_K` is over K, returns constrained
   arrays, by value or through a hidden pointer, not on the secondary
   stack. Probe: 0.18 s against 0.59 s, and one lemma serves every
   caller. This removes the stack-base group, 44%.
2. **No recursion where the Cryptol has none.** A literal or a word of
   `split` is an iterated aggregate (`Literal_Agg`: one proof of 0.78 s
   for a chain of 65 lemmas, about 40 to 55 s).
3. **Keep the Cryptol's recursions, but let SAW unfold the linear ones**
   (a script change: `Rounds.Foldl` 2.4 s against 23.4 s; the cipher
   folds likewise); keep per-index lemmas for `W`.
4. **Bits packed into bytes, or a word as a word.** Cryptol's `[32]` is
   one value; `Value_Packed` is 15 to 24× cheaper than one Boolean per
   byte.
5. **No null concatenations** (a base case at width 1): no lax load, and
   overflow proved by SAW as well as gnatprove.

For translation equivalence the lesson is the same: the cost of checking
two programs equal by symbolic execution follows their *structural*
distance, not their size. Where both sides loop over fixed-size arrays of
words (the implementation here, and C), one proof per function suffices;
each unconstrained result, unshared recursion and bit-per-byte sequence on
the SPARK side multiplies the proofs, and SAW's contracts must then be
written per caller. A SPARK translation meant to be checked against C is
cheapest when it keeps C's data sizes and iteration structure and states
its readability in contracts and names, not in a different
representation.

## Not measured

* The long record (`SAW_LONG=1`) and HMAC at (0, 0), where z3 runs
  886 s on a constant goal: not re-run.
* The cipher folds unfolded (by analogy with `Rounds.Foldl`, not run).
* A spec layer rewritten along the five points above: no change to
  `spark/src` (the issue asks for the explanation, and gnatprove's proofs
  of those units would have to be redone); the probes measure each point
  on its own.
