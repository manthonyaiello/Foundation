# Measurements: why SAW costs so much more on the spec layer (#69)

Measured 2026-10-21/22 on the x86_64 IOD (`ip-10-10-103-187`: 8 CPUs,
15.7 GB, no swap), on `main` = `09fa337` (PR #93's merge). The SAW record
`spark/saw/results.txt` is of `937e485`; nothing SAW reads changed since.
Tools as in `results.txt`: llvm-gcc 27.0 (LLVM 21.1.8), SAW 1.6, Cryptol
3.6.0, z3 4.8.14, SAW's yices.

**How.** Each experiment is a SAW script, `e_<name>.saw`, and, for the
experiments that compare two ways of writing one function, the probe
units `probes/*.ads` (not part of the crate; never in `spark/src`), all
under `spark/saw/experiments/issue-69/`. `run.sh` runs one against the
bitcode `check-saw.sh` builds, in a scratch copy of `spark/saw/`'s
scripts, optionally with one `sed` variant applied (`variants/`), capped
and timed (`/usr/bin/time -v`: wall-clock and peak RSS). `run-all.sh` runs
them all, one at a time. No recorded script changes, so `results.txt`
stays current; **no new SAW record was made, and the long record
(`results-long.txt`) was not re-run** (neither was needed: nothing SAW
reads changed). The helpers `causes.py` and `pairs.py` read a record;
`profile_summary.py` reads crucible's profile.

Two tactics appear only in the experiments, never in a recorded script:
`admit` (it accepts the goal without a solver, so a proof timed with it
is symbolic execution, override matching and goal construction alone; the
solver's share is the difference) and crucible's profiler.
